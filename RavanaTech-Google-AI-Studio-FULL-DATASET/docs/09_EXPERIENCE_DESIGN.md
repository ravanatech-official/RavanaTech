# 09 — Experience Design Rules

## The 20-client / 20-needs principle

Twenty visitors may have twenty completely different reasons for arriving. The interface therefore behaves like a receptionist, not a brochure.

The visitor should never be expected to:
- read every page;
- understand Ravana Tech's internal service taxonomy;
- know technical terms;
- remember information from a previous screen;
- type the same details twice;
- guess what to click next;
- search a long FAQ for an answer.

## Every click must pay the visitor back

After every meaningful click, one of these must happen:

1. **Clarity increases** — “Now I understand.”
2. **Effort decreases** — “I don't have to figure this out.”
3. **Proof appears** — “I can see what this means.”
4. **Progress becomes visible** — “We are getting somewhere.”
5. **A useful action becomes one click away** — “I know what to do next.”

If a UI element does not create one of these outcomes, remove it.

## The 50–75% value-before-contact rule

By the time a visitor leaves, the experience should ideally have already delivered a substantial portion of the useful work:
- their need was translated into plain language;
- the relevant Ravana service was identified;
- a relevant demo/proof was shown when useful;
- the important project decisions were captured;
- a simple project direction/brief was generated;
- the next action is obvious.

The visitor should never feel that the entire website was a sales funnel ending in “fill this form”.

## Never trap the visitor

“Force the next step” means **guide**, not manipulate.

Every guided step should provide:
- one obvious primary action;
- Back;
- Change my answer;
- Not sure / Help me;
- Ravana Agent.

## Founder presence ratio

The founder appears at the moments where a human receptionist would speak:
- arrival;
- acknowledgement;
- reassurance;
- important transition;
- result/handoff;
- thank-you.

Do not make the founder avatar narrate every tiny click. That becomes tiring rather than personal.

## Accessibility

The same meaning must always exist as visible text. Voice is an enhancement, not the only way to understand the interface. Keyboard navigation, readable contrast, captions, reduced-motion support, and mobile layouts are mandatory.

## Satisfaction measurement

Do not infer trust from “time on page”. Ask the visitor directly at the right moment and combine that with non-identifying journey friction metrics.

The business question is:

> “Did Ravana Tech make the visitor's job easier?”

That is the metric the architecture is built around.
