Our Story — Hammer & Hops

WE DIDN'T
BUY A BREWERY.
WE BUILT ONE.

In 2012, Portland's industrial district had one more empty machine shop, oil on the floor, broken windows, a 40-ton press still bolted to the concrete. We saw tanks. My brother Eli and I had spent three years brewing out of a garage in St. Johns, hauling wort in buckets, trading bottles for welding lessons.

The shop became Ironworks. We kept the crane rails, the soot, the name someone had spray-painted on the back wall in 1978. We rebuilt the brewhouse from salvaged dairy equipment — that's why the lauter tun has that dent on the side.

2012 Garage Batch #1 — Smoked Rye
Burnt. Undrinkable. Kept the yeast alive though.
2014 Lease Signed on Machine Shop
15 BBL system installed with a forklift and six friends. No permit for the forklift.
Today 47 Barrels, 9 Taps, One Wood-Fired Pizza Oven
Still hammering. Still learning. Come taste what patience does.
Founder — Eli Mercer
"If you can fix a lathe, you can fix a stuck mash. It's all just patience and listening to the metal."

How We Brew — No Shortcuts

IRON, OAK,
TIME & GRAIN.

Our process is stubbornly manual. We mill by hand the night before, we watch the boil, we taste from barrels weekly. It's inefficient. It's the point.

100%
Floor Malted
0
Adjuncts Ever
01

Mill & Mash —
By Feel

We roller mill our own grain in the old tool crib. Oregon pale, Munich, a touch of rye. Decoction when the malt calls for it. No computers — just iodine and intuition.

02:00 AM — Milling Starts
02

Ferment &
Forget

House ale yeast from a 2014 bottle of Orval, plus wild cultures caught in our barrel room. Primary in steel, secondary wherever makes sense — oak, foeder, bottle.

3 Weeks — 18 Months — Depends
03

Pour Without
Apology

We carbonate low, serve cool but not cold, in proper glassware. No frosted mugs. If we aged it 12 months, you should taste 12 months.

Taste. Talk. Trade.
