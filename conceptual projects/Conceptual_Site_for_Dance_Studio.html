flūid began as a winter sublet. A 900 sq ft room with cracked mirrors and a single barre. No logo. No schedule. Just a group message: “floor is warm, who’s coming?”

Fig.02 — Northern Light BARRE STUDY

The studio keeps its original maple. Sanded once a year. We mop with cold water only. The ink-black barres were salvaged from a closed ballet school in Queens — 1972, still solid.

Contemporary is often taught as choreography. We start with physics. How does your scapula slide when you reach? What happens to your breath if you suspend a fall three counts longer? The answer is never in the mirror. It's in the floor, in the other body, in the sound of weight shifting.

Classes are 90 minutes. No levels, but clear invitations. Beginners work next to professionals because curiosity is not hierarchical.
