侘wabi

Acceptance of transience and imperfection.

Our clay is mixed with grog from old failed pots. Ground back into the new. Nothing wasted. Every vessel contains the memory of the last one that cracked in the kiln. That's wabi — humble, unpretentious, finding beauty in the bare.

寂sabi

Beauty that comes with age and use.

We want our mugs to chip. To have tea stains in the crazing lines after 6 years. Sabi is the bloom, not the flaw. Our glaze breaks over edges to reveal the iron body beneath — like skin over bone.

“At first I wanted it perfectly round. Now I ask for the one that leans a little.” — Mari, regular since 2019

Akari Sato
founder / potter

Akari learned in Mashiko, Japan for 3 years with a 78-year-old master who never measured. “If you need calipers, you are not listening to the clay.” She opened Tsuchi in a railway arch in 2018 with a second-hand Shimpo wheel and a rescue dog.

02 — Process

We work in circles.
The clay does too.

1

Wedging / remembering

20 minutes of push, fold, spiral. Removing air, aligning particles. It’s like kneading a memory back into the body. If you rush this, everything collapses later.

2

Centering / listening

Both hands, water, breathe. You are not forcing it center, you are asking. When true center comes, the wheel goes quiet. Beginners chase this sound for months.

3

Opening / risk

Two thumbs. A hole that becomes a bottom. Too thin and it tears. Too thick and it never dries. Every pot holds this moment of near-failure.

4

Pulling / breathing

Inside hand, outside hand, lift. The wall rises like a lung. We teach you to leave it a little thicker at the rim — so it lives after you put it down.

The kiln has final say.

We fire with wood on full moons. Soda at peak. You do not control glaze. You invite it. About 30% will have a story you didn’t plan — those are the ones we keep.
