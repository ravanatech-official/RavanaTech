ISSUE: WHY WE STILL CUT & PASTE IN 2025
SOFIA • OWNER / BUYER / MENDER
Runs the shop with her chihuahua Pickle and has not bought new clothes since 2019.

I started this place with $800 and a storage unit in Queens. No business plan. Just a pile of men's workshirts I rescued from a shuttered factory upstate and a dream that maybe people were tired of looking like everyone else on their FYP.

The first year I lived on the floor in the back. You can still see the coffee stain on the floorboard behind the denim rack — that’s from the night I got the call about the massive estate sale in Montclair. 400 pieces, 1930s-1990s, all from one woman. A former costume designer for Broadway who kept everything.

→ RULES WE ACTUALLY LIVE BY:
  • ✦ NO HAULS OVER 10 ITEMS. YOU DON'T NEED THAT MUCH.
  • ✦ IF IT DOESN'T FIT, WE TAILOR. CLOTHES SHOULD FIT YOU.
  • ✦ EVERY PIECE $100+ GETS A HANDWRITTEN HISTORY CARD.
  • ✦ WE PAY 40% CASH FOR YOUR GOOD STUFF (NO SHEIN PLS).

People ask if it's sustainable. Sure, but that's not why we do it. We do it because new clothes feel dead. They haven't lived. The best leather jacket in here was broken in at a motorcycle rally in '78, then worn by a lesbian librarian in Park Slope for 20 years. It has stories.

We repair, we re-dye, we re-imagine. That weird pink slip dress with the hand-painted flowers? That was a stained bridesmaid dress I found for $4. Now it's $120 and someone's going to get married in it. That's the magic.

Look, I know thrifting is trendy now. But this isn't a trend for us. This is just how we get dressed in the morning. Come get weird with us.
