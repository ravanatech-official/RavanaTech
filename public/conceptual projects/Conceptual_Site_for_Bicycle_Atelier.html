PERCHÈ
ACCIAIO

Carbon is fast.
Aluminum is cheap.
Steel is forever.

We learned from Eraldo, who learned from Mino, who learned from his father. Three generations of file marks on the same workbench.

BenchEst. 1972
Steel remembers every road. It doesn't die, it patinas with you.

A lugged frame is not nostalgia. It's engineering you can see. Each lug is cast, then filed by hand for 6 hours until the line disappears. No hidden glue. No autoclave. Just silver, brass, and heat. We use Reynolds 853 air-hardening and Columbus Spirit because they get stronger at the joint where cheap tubes fail. A Brenta frame is built to be re-painted three times. To be passed on. To be crashed and re-aligned on the same jig it was born on.

01

MITRE & FILE

Tubes cut on a 1974 Harrison lathe, mitred to 0.1°, filed until the junction disappears when you run your thumb over it. No gaps for brass to hide in.

02

BRAZE IN JIG

72 hours in the Anvil jig under tension. Silver at 620°C for lugs, brass at 870°C for filetto. Frame goes from orange to straw to nothing. We know by sound.

03

ALLINEA & VERIFICA

Cold set, checked, faced, chased. Every thread cut twice. Then it hangs for 24h. If it moves, we start over. That's why we have a lifetime warranty.
