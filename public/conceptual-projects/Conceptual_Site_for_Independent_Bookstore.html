Paris, 1971 — Archive

Paragraph was never meant to be efficient. It was meant to be precise — a shop where a clerk could tell you, from memory, who else bought this book and why they cried on page 84.

Founded by Elena March, a translator who smuggled editions of Baldwin and Lorca back from New York in a false-bottom suitcase, the shop has occupied the same tilting building on Rue des Écoles for fifty-three years. The shelves were built from reclaimed pews of Saint-Étienne-du-Mont. The heating still rattles.

We still write receipts by hand. We still let you sit on the floor. We still argue about translations at lunch.
